# E10 — Training dynamics (why objectives help or not)

Visual evidence for the mechanism behind the objective ablation (E3/E8): blunt
objectives saturate within a few epochs, while useful objectives keep decreasing
throughout training. Per-objective SSL loss over epochs, from the E3 single-objective
runs (each run trains one objective, so its loss curve is clean).

**Source:** `plot_e10_loss_curves.py` → `ogbn_benchmarks/results/e10_loss_curves.png`

## Figure — two panels, x capped at 400 epochs

- **Left (feature objectives):** centroid, stats, recon plunge to a small floor by
  ~epoch 20 and stay flat thereafter.
- **Right (structural / label objectives):** path and hist start high and keep
  decreasing well past epoch 200.

## Reading

By epoch 20 the feature objectives have reached their floor and provide no further
gradient for the remaining training; their targets are low-variance and quickly fit.
The structural and label objectives sit at a much higher loss and continue to
decrease, i.e.\ they keep posing informative supervision. This is the temporal
signature of the objective ablation: an objective that saturates early cannot shape
the representation beyond the first few epochs, whereas one whose loss keeps
decreasing continues to do so.

## Framing for the paper

Use as the mechanism figure accompanying the objective analysis: the loss dynamics
make visible, in a way a final-metric table cannot, why low-variance feature
objectives are inert and why the label/structural objectives keep contributing.
