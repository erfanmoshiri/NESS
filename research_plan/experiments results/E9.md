# E9 — SSL benefit by feature type (dense-embedding vs. binary BoW)

Does neighborhood self-supervision help on BINARY bag-of-words graphs the way it
does on dense-embedding arxiv? Run hist / anchorcls WITH vs WITHOUT on the four
small binary datasets, locked config, MCAR @ 80%.

**Source:** `experiments/e9_ssl_by_feature.sh` →
`ogbn_benchmarks/results/e9_sslfeature_20260724_060959/`.

**Status:** DONE (single-seed).

## Results — Test Macro-F1 @ 80% (binary BoW)

| dataset | no_ssl | hist | Δhist | anchorcls | Δanchorcls |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cora | 0.7976 | 0.7972 | −0.000 | 0.7865 | −0.011 |
| citeseer | 0.5947 | 0.5962 | +0.002 | 0.6014 | +0.007 |
| amac | 0.8630 | 0.8580 | −0.005 | 0.8599 | −0.003 |
| amap | 0.8763 | 0.8763 | +0.000 | 0.8811 | +0.005 |

**Reference — dense-embedding (arxiv) @ 80%, from E3:** hist **+0.018** over no_ssl.

## Finding

**SSL is inert on binary bag-of-words graphs.** Every delta is within ±0.01 (noise);
no dataset shows a consistent benefit — including `hist`, the objective that helps
on arxiv (+0.018). Neighborhood-centric self-supervision helps on **dense-embedding**
graphs but **not** on sparse binary attributes.

**Mechanism:** neighborhood-statistic targets require averaging neighbor features.
On dense continuous embeddings this yields a meaningful, predictable target; on
sparse 0/1 bag-of-words vectors the average is near-noise, so there is nothing
learnable for the objective to teach.

## Why this matters (the "why arxiv" justification, now evidenced)

- Establishes the **feature-type boundary** of the contribution: SSL operates in the
  dense-embedding regime. This is the empirical basis for focusing on large
  dense-embedding graphs (arxiv) rather than the small binary benchmarks common in
  prior work (and in our own previous paper, which used exactly these binary graphs).
- Pairs with **E12** (SSL benefit grows with missingness) to fully map SSL's operating
  regime: **helps iff (dense-embedding features) AND (scarce observations)**; inert on
  binary attributes; redundant when features are plentiful.
- SSL is *inert*, not *harmful*, on binary graphs → keeps the framing "regime-dependent,"
  not "SSL is bad."

**Caveat:** single-seed; the ±0.01 deltas are within noise (which is precisely the
point — no signal). The contrast with arxiv's +0.018 is the load-bearing comparison.
