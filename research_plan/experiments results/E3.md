## Story for the paper

1. **The combined objective set (hist+path) is best (+0.022 over no-SSL)** and is the
   locked default. It outperforms every single objective, so pairing a label-based with
   a structural objective is beneficial.
2. **Label-awareness helps.** hist (+0.012) is among the strongest single objectives,
   and the best set (hist+path) is anchored by it. This supports the finding that
   class-aware objectives are the most reliable (kept as a semi-supervised component;
   no labels needed at inference, see note below).
3. **The two-view / contrastive structure contributes.** single_view is the worst
   configuration (−0.009 vs no_ssl), so removing the second view and contrastive term
   hurts. This justifies retaining the contrastive component.
4. Remaining single objectives (path, centroid, anchor, recon) give small positive
   lifts; triplet and stats are near-neutral. On the well-configured encoder the
   individual objectives are close, and the clearest gains come from combining a
   label-based and a structural objective.
5. **Feature reconstruction (recon) is among the better single objectives (+0.017).**
   This is consistent with our prior work [NCSSL], where a feature-based objective helped
   because the goal was feature reconstruction; the same signal remains useful here as an
   auxiliary loss. (One-sentence cite when writing the .tex.)

## Probe: target class-relevance vs. downstream lift (Figure A)

For each per-node-target objective we fit a logistic-regression probe to predict the
node class from the objective's target, then pair its probe macro-F1 (how class-relevant
the target is) with its downstream lift. Relational objectives (path, triplet) have no
per-node target vector and are excluded.

**Source:** `probe_objectives.py` → `results/probe_objectives.json`; figure
`plot_figA_probe_vs_lift.py` → `results/figA_probe_vs_lift.pdf`.

| Objective | Probe macro-F1 (class-relevance) | Δ F1 (downstream lift) |
|---|---|---|
| hist | 0.474 | +0.020 |
| centroid | 0.356 | +0.007 |
| recon | 0.219 | +0.017 |
| anchor | 0.141 | +0.004 |
| stats | 0.086 | −0.003 |

**Finding:** the more class-relevant an objective's target, the more it helps downstream.
hist has the most class-relevant target and the largest lift; stats has the least
class-relevant target and is the only objective that hurts. This supports the claim that
**class-relevance of the target is what makes an objective useful**, and explains why the
label-aware histogram is the most reliable objective.

## Note for the paper — `hist` is semi-supervised, not label-free (and needs NO labels at test)

Some objectives are **label-free** (purely structural): `path`, `triplet`, `anchor`
predict quantities derived only from graph topology and node embeddings — they need
no labels, ever. In contrast, `hist` predicts the **class distribution of a node's
observable neighbors**, so it uses labels during training. This raises a fair question:
*if `hist` relies on labels, does it need labels at inference?*

**No.** All self-supervised objectives — `hist` included — are **training-only
auxiliary losses**. They shape the encoder's weights during training and are then
discarded. The inference path is only `z = encoder(x) → classifier(z) → prediction`;
the hist head, path head, edge decoder, and contrastive term are never evaluated at
test time. No labels are required at inference (beyond the ground-truth test labels
used solely to *score* F1/Acc).

**No leakage.** The `hist` target is built from **observable-node labels only**: the
label one-hot matrix is populated exclusively for observable nodes, so masked/val/test
nodes contribute zero to every neighbor-histogram. This is the same label access the
classification loss already uses — i.e. standard *semi-supervised* training, not a
leak of held-out labels.

**Consequence for framing.** `path`/`triplet`/`anchor` are label-free and thus apply
even when no labels exist anywhere. `hist` is **semi-supervised**: it depends on
training-time observable labels (which we have anyway) but needs **no labels at
inference**. We therefore report `hist` as a semi-supervised comparison point, and keep
the *label-free* structural objectives as the core of the method.
