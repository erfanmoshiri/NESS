# E7 — View Analysis (RQ4)

Vary the second contrastive view, objective fixed to hist+path. Which view best
supports the shared representation. arxiv MCAR @ 80%, locked config (SAGE-3L-128,
w_cls=3, dropout 0.3), 800 epochs.

**Source:** `experiments/e7_view_ablation.sh` → `ogbn_benchmarks/results/e7_viewablation_20260726_114117/`.

**Status:** DONE (locked config).

## Findings for the paper

1. **Simple edge-masking is the best view.** edge_mask (0.449) is the top view, ahead of
   ppr diffusion (0.436, +0.013) and well ahead of the elaborate views (deep 0.423,
   feat_mask 0.405). Richer view construction does not improve, and often harms,
   downstream performance. We adopt edge-mask as the default second view.

2. **Structure-perturbing views beat feature-perturbing views.** The strongest views
   (edge_mask, ppr) perturb graph structure; the feature-perturbing view (feat_mask) is
   the weakest. This mirrors the objective axis in E3: structure-based signal is more
   useful than feature-based signal for shaping the representation.

3. **View choice matters, but simplicity wins within the structural family.** The spread
   from best to worst view is meaningful (0.449 to 0.405), so the view is a real design
   axis; among reasonable choices, the simplest structural perturbation is best.

## Takeaways
- Use **edge_mask** as the default view (simplest and best).
- RQ4: structure-perturbing views outperform feature-perturbing ones; complexity does not
  help. Pairs with the E3 finding that the two-view/contrastive structure contributes
  (single_view is the weakest configuration there).
