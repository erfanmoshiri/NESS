# E12 — SSL benefit grows with missingness (RQ2 × RQ3)

**Key finding:** self-supervision helps more as features become scarcer. NESS
(hist+path) and the no-SSL baseline both degrade with missingness, but the baseline
degrades faster, so the SSL advantage widens.

NESS, ogbn-arxiv, MCAR, locked config (SAGE-3L-128, edge_mask, w_cls=3, dropout 0.3),
800 epochs. Test macro-F1.

**Source:** `experiments/e_ssl_vs_missingness.sh` →
`ogbn_benchmarks/results/e_sslmiss_20260726_144148/`.
Figure: `plot_e12_missingness.py` → `results/e12_missingness.pdf`.

**Status:** DONE (locked config).

## Results — Test macro-F1

| Config | @0.8 | @0.9 | @0.95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| no_ssl | 0.4425 | 0.4074 | 0.3500 |
| hist+path (NESS) | 0.4479 | 0.4165 | 0.3809 |
| **Δ (hist+path − no_ssl)** | **+0.005** | **+0.009** | **+0.031** |

(anchor was also run but is erratic here — below no_ssl at 0.8, slightly above at
0.9/0.95 — so it is omitted from the figure/story; the clean, monotone result is
no_ssl vs hist+path.)

## Reading

- The SSL advantage grows monotonically with missingness: +0.005 → +0.009 → +0.031.
- At $0.95$ missing, hist+path lifts F1 from 0.350 to 0.381 (+0.031), a clear gain.
- Mechanism (interpretive): when observed features are abundant the encoder already
  recovers most of the signal, so SSL adds little; as the graph becomes information-
  starved, the self-supervised objectives supply an increasingly large share of the
  usable signal.

## Framing for the paper (Section 11 / \ref{sec:exp-missingness-regime})
This is the missingness-severity axis of the "when does SSL help" story: SSL is most
valuable under severe missingness. Pairs with E9 (feature-type axis) to give the two
conditions under which self-supervision helps.

## Note
Run-to-run variation: no_ssl @0.8 is 0.4425 here vs 0.4242 in the E3 run (~0.018,
same config/rate). The load-bearing result is the widening trend, not any single point.
