# E12 — SSL benefit grows with missingness (RQ2 × RQ3)

**The key finding: self-supervision helps most precisely when features are scarcest.**
At moderate missingness the strong encoder (FP-prefill + SAGE) leaves little headroom,
so SSL is marginal; as missingness increases the encoder starves and SSL fills the gap.

NESS, ogbn-arxiv, MCAR, SAGE-128, edge_mask, 800 epochs. Test Macro-F1.
(@0.8 row from E3; @0.9 and @0.95 from the ssl-vs-missingness probe.)

**Source:** `experiments/e_ssl_vs_missingness.sh` → `ogbn_benchmarks/results/e_sslmiss_20260723_154349/`
(@0.9, @0.95 rows). @0.8 row from E3's `e3_ablation_20260723_094037/`.

| Config | @0.8 | @0.9 | @0.95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| no_ssl | 0.3748 | 0.3390 | 0.3043 |
| anchor | 0.3860 (+0.011) | 0.3481 (+0.009) | 0.3063 (+0.002) |
| **hist+path** | **0.3906 (+0.016)** | **0.3552 (+0.016)** | **0.3321 (+0.028)** |

## Reading

- **hist+path's advantage over no_ssl GROWS with missingness:** +0.016 → +0.016 → **+0.028**.
  At 95% missing, SSL lifts F1 from 0.304 → 0.332 (~+9% relative) — a clear, non-marginal gain.
- **anchor alone fades** at extreme missingness (+0.011 → +0.002): a single structural
  objective isn't enough when features are nearly gone; the label-aware `hist` in the combo
  is what sustains the gain.
- This is the honest resolution to "why doesn't SSL help much?" — it *does*, but only where
  there is headroom. At 80% the FP+SAGE encoder already captures most of the signal.

## Why this matters for the paper

- **RQ2 story:** the method's advantage widens as the problem gets harder — the money-shot
  framing, now with SSL (not just the encoder) contributing at the hard end.
- **RQ3 story:** reframes the objective finding honestly — SSL objectives are *redundant with
  a strong encoder at moderate missingness* and *become essential under severe scarcity*.
  This is a more defensible claim than "our objective always wins."
